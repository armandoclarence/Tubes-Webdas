故宫博物院珍藏

The Palace Museum Collections

Housing over 1.8 million historical artifacts, the museum serves as the ultimate sanctuary for China's most profound artistic and cultural heritage.

From Imperial Sanctuary to Global Heritage

For over 500 years, the treasures of the Forbidden City were strictly reserved for the eyes of the Emperor and his inner court. It wasn't until 1925 that these gates were opened to the public. Today, the Palace Museum holds the most comprehensive collection of Chinese material culture in the world, representing the pinnacle of ancient craftsmanship.

The collections are categorized into specialized divisions, preserving the evolution of Chinese aesthetics across dynasties. From the pristine glaze of Song Dynasty porcelain to the intricate silk threads of Qing dragon robes, these artifacts tell the story of a civilization obsessed with harmony, nature, and cosmological order.

瓷
Ceramics
Porcelain Arts

The Alchemy of Earth & Fire

China's name is synonymous with fine porcelain. The museum holds over 350,000 ceramic pieces, charting a continuous timeline from Neolithic pottery to the sophisticated imperial kilns of Jingdezhen.

Highlights include the ultra-rare Ru ware (汝窑) from the Song Dynasty, known for its pale "sky-after-rain" blue glaze and subtle crackles, as well as the world-famous Blue-and-White (青花瓷) porcelain of the Ming Dynasty, painted with cobalt imported via the Silk Road.

玉
Jade
Stone Carvings

The Stone of Heaven

In Chinese culture, Jade (Yu) is valued higher than gold, symbolizing purity, moral integrity, and immortality. The imperial collection features over 30,000 jade artifacts.

The collection includes massive jade mountains carved with landscape scenes, delicate burial suits from ancient eras, and most importantly, the Imperial Seals (玉玺). These massive, intricately carved square seals were stamped on state documents to project the absolute authority of the Emperor's "Mandate of Heaven."

画
Paintings
Ink & Brush

Capturing the Spirit Resonance

Traditional Chinese painting does not aim for strict photorealism, but rather seeks to capture the inner energy (Qi) of the subject. The museum safeguards ancient silk scrolls that are incredibly fragile and only exhibited rarely to protect them from light degradation.

One of the most legendary pieces in the collection is "Along the River During the Qingming Festival" (清明上河图). This massive panoramic scroll captures the bustling daily life, commerce, and architecture of the Song Dynasty capital with astonishing microscopic detail.

丝
Silk
Textile Arts

The Threads of the Dragon

China introduced sericulture (silk production) to the world. The imperial wardrobe department created garments of unimaginable complexity, utilizing gold threads, peacock feathers, and pearls woven directly into the fabric.

The ultimate expression of this art is the Dragon Robe (龙袍). Worn exclusively by the Emperor, these bright yellow silk robes were embroidered with nine majestic five-clawed dragons hovering over waves and clouds, representing the ruler's dominion over the earth and the heavens.

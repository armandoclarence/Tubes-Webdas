宫殿建筑

Sacred Architecture

An architectural blueprint exploring the master structures, interlocking engineering, and structural numerology of the Imperial Court.

01 /

The Central Symmetrical Meridian Axis

The layout of the Forbidden City stands as a physical manifestation of traditional Chinese cosmic order, aligned precisely with Beijing’s ancient 8-kilometer central symmetrical meridian axis. All principal palaces line up perfectly along this path to symbolize the Emperor as the stable center of the cosmos. Dimensions were measured using the sacred Lu Ban ruler to ensure total environmental harmony.

02 /

The Elemental Hierarchy of Imperial Colors

The palette chosen follows a strict symbolic system based on Five Elements theory. Crimson red walls embody fire, warmth, and protective fortune. Roof tiles are clad exclusively in yellow glazed glass — representing earth and the center of the elemental cycle — reserved for the Emperor to denote supreme authority.

03 /

Mythical Statuettes & Spatial Structural Rank

Perched along the ridge lines of the palace roofs are rows of carefully sculpted mythical animal guardians. The total count directly corresponds to the building's strategic rank. The Hall of Supreme Harmony features ten distinct beast statuettes, highlighting its status as the highest seat of power.

04 /

Dougong Joinery: Elastic Seismic Defense

These monumental wooden pavilions were built completely without using a single metallic nail. Master carpenters relied on Dougong — an intricate system of interlocking wooden brackets. This flexible joinery acts as an elastic seismic shock absorber, allowing the massive frames to withstand over 200 severe earthquakes safely.

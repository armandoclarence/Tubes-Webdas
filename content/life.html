宫廷生活

Daily Life in the Court

An intimate exploration into the domestic rituals, societal hierarchies, and private daily dynamics of the imperial household.

Behind its imposing ceremonial facades, the Forbidden City sheltered an intricate, deeply insular daily court life. The Inner Palaces formed a private world for thousands of imperial consorts and concubines who lived in immense luxury, yet navigated subtle internal rivalries to secure favors from the line. Attending to them were the royal eunuchs—highly trusted servants who managed everything from internal household affairs to state administration secrets.

Fascinatingly, the imperial family held a deep affection for animal companions; they bred purebred Pekingese dogs provided with dedicated palace quarters and specialized caretakers, and protected hundreds of friendly court cats—whose descendants still freely roam and safeguard the Forbidden City today.

The Palace and The Hutongs: A Shared Urban Reality

While the Emperor ruled from the absolute isolation of the inner courts, the pulse of the capital thrived just outside the walls in the **Hutongs**—narrow lanes formed by lines of traditional courtyard houses (Siheyuan). This contrast created a clear societal hierarchy: the massive layout of the Forbidden City reflected divine cosmic order, while the close-knit alleys of the Hutongs represented the grounding everyday life of old Beijing. Today, both structures stand together as vital guardians of Chinese cultural identity.

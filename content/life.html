The Inner Court & Daily Imperial Life

An intimate exploration of the private residential quarters, hierarchical social structures, and the daily human ecosystem that kept the Forbidden City running.

Beyond the Throne: The Invisible Metropolis

While the magnificent Outer Court was dedicated entirely to state affairs and grand ceremonial displays, the northern section of the Forbidden City—known as the Inner Court (内廷, Nèitíng)—was the highly restricted domestic realm of the Emperor.

This was a self-sustaining microcosm populated by thousands of individuals. Aside from the Emperor himself, the only adult males permitted to stay overnight within these walls were the eunuchs. It was a world governed by intense hierarchy, strict sumptuary laws dictating everything from silk colors to meal portions, and a surprising dedication to leisure and animal companionship.

后
The Harem
Inner Court Hierarchy

Empress, Consorts & Concubines

The imperial harem was an incredibly complex bureaucratic institution rather than just a royal family. At the absolute apex stood the Empress (皇后), the supreme mother of the state who commanded the central palaces.

Below her, strict numerical quotas governed the ranks: Imperial Noble Consorts, Noble Consorts, Consorts, Imperial Concubines, Noble Ladies, and lower-ranking attendants. Their monthly stipends of silver, silk, pork, and even the number of eunuchs assigned to serve them were rigidly correlated to their rank and their ability to bear male heirs.

太
The Eunuchs
Administrative Backbone

The Architects of Palace Logistics

Eunuchs (太监, Tàijiān) were the indispensable administrative backbone of the Forbidden City. At the height of the Ming Dynasty, up to 70,000 eunuchs were employed across the empire, with thousands living directly inside the palace.

They handled everything from cooking and cleaning to managing the imperial treasury, drafting edicts, and running the Imperial Pharmacy. Because they lacked physical heirs, emperors often trusted them with immense political power, leading to legendary (and sometimes catastrophic) historical periods of "Eunuch Dictatorships."

宠
Royal Pets
Canines & Felines

Pekingese Dogs & Palace Cats

The Forbidden City was home to highly pampered royal pets. The Pekingese dog (京巴) was bred specifically to resemble miniature guardian lions. They were so revered that commoners were legally forbidden to own them, and they were often carried in the wide sleeves of imperial robes.

Cats were also beloved companions, particularly during the Ming Dynasty, which even established an official "Cat Management Office." Today, the Palace Museum is famously guarded by over 200 "Royal Cats"—many of which are direct descendants of the original feline companions of the Qing emperors.

医
Imperial Medicine
Traditional Healing

The Imperial Medical Institute

The health of the Emperor was a matter of national security. The Taiyiyuan (太医院) was the supreme medical authority, staffed by the finest physicians in the empire.

Treatment relied on Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), utilizing acupuncture, moxibustion, and highly guarded herbal formulas containing ginseng, deer horn, and cordyceps. Diagnosing the Emperor was highly ritualized; physicians often had to read his pulse by placing their fingers on a silk thread tied to his wrist, avoiding direct physical contact with his sacred body.

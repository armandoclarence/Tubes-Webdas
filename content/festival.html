Imperial Festivals

Life within the Forbidden City was governed by a strict ritual calendar tied to astronomical cycles, moon phases, and solar alignments.

Because traditional Chinese festivals operate on a **lunisolar system**, their calendar dates vary annually on the Gregorian solar calendar. This timing ensures that each celebration harmonizes perfectly with seasonal cycles. Major milestones featured spectacular processions with thousands of guards parading across the courtyard plazas. Each celebration concluded with formal Royal Banquets accompanied by traditional court music and classical Chinese opera performances.

Month 1, Day 1
📜 The Spring Festival

The grandest imperial celebration. The Emperor brushes calligraphy to bestow luck, dragon dances fill the plazas, and gold silk lanterns illuminate the corridors.

Month 1, Day 15
🏮 The Lantern Festival

Concluding the New Year celebrations, thousands of gold silk lanterns are lit along the corridors to celebrate cosmic peace, reconciliation, and the light of seasonal renewal.

Month 5, Day 5
🐉 Dragon Boat Festival

An energetic celebration featuring competitive dragon boat racing rowed to the beat of thunderous drums, held alongside medicinal herb balancing rituals and sticky rice dumplings (Zongzi).
